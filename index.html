THE MINT
MADE A PROMISE.
DID IT KEEP IT?

Ecash is digital cash issued by a mint that holds bitcoin for it. A mint can sign a promise to count each payment it issues in its books. SOLVENT checks that the promise was kept — in the mint’s signed books, on public records, and against bitcoin it can see on chain — before you accept the ecash.

The problem

A valid Cashu token
doesn’t prove the mint
counted what it owes.

Cashu can prove that ecash was signed by a mint. That alone does not prove the mint included that issuance in its liabilities — or that its reserves still cover the liabilities it has committed to. A mint can issue valid ecash while omitting that obligation from its accounting.

✓ mint signature

but

Liability

? counted or omitted

Reserve

? enough or not

The gap

Wallets need a way to check the mint’s obligation before accepting the token.

In plain words

Five words you will see.

Cashu proof
The ecash token you received. The mint’s signature shows it is genuine; whether it is still unspent and redeemable is something only the mint can answer when you spend it.
Receipt
The mint’s signed promise to count your issuance in a specific accounting period.
Epoch
One accounting period. When it closes, the mint signs a summary of the liabilities it has recorded.
Liability commitment
That signed summary: a total plus a structure (a sum-MMR) proving exactly which issuances it includes.
Reserve
Bitcoin the verifier looks up on chain itself, instead of taking the mint’s word for it.

The solution

SOLVENT makes
the mint’s promise
checkable.

Each issuance gets a signed receipt. Before acceptance, SOLVENT checks it against the mint’s closed accounting, the public record and the reserve — and blocks acceptance when any part is missing, inconsistent, stale or underfunded.

  1. Issuance
  2. Signed liability receipt
  3. Closed accounting state
  4. Public Nostr evidence
  5. Live Bitcoin reserve
  6. Accept / Refuse

Every check runs in your browser, on data anyone can fetch: the mint’s own signatures, public Nostr relays and the Bitcoin chain. No SOLVENT server is trusted for the verdict.

What SOLVENT catches

A signed promise
should be checkable.

If the epoch a receipt promised closes without that issuance, the contradiction is in the mint’s own signatures — and SOLVENT refuses on it, even when everything else looks healthy.

  • The receipt can be validly signed
  • The promised epoch can really close
  • The epoch’s own signed manifest can look internally consistent
  • The promised issuance can still be missing from it

The other half

Inclusion alone
is not enough.

Even when an issuance really is counted in a closed epoch, that epoch’s mint still needs real Bitcoin reserve to cover the liabilities it committed to. SOLVENT checks both independently — accounting honesty and reserve coverage — before it will ever accept.

  • Accounting honesty: was the promise kept?
  • Reserve coverage: does the observed reserve cover the committed liabilities?
  • Both must pass, or SOLVENT refuses

An observed, unspent reserve that covers the committed liabilities does not prove the reserve backs only this mint, that liabilities outside the commitment don’t exist, future solvency, or that a redemption will succeed. Trust model →

Four checks. One acceptance gate.

SOLVENT gates ecash
on the real decision chain.

01 / Origin

Was this ecash really issued?

Cashu / NUT-12 DLEQ validation, holder-reconstructed B′ — never a mint-supplied ID.

02 / Promise

What did the mint promise?

Verify the signed Proof-of-Liabilities receipt and the epoch it promised to count this issuance in.

03 / Accounting

Did the closed epoch keep the promise?

Verify sum-MMR inclusion for the exact reconstructed issuance against the signed epoch manifest.

04 / Reserve

Does the observed reserve cover the committed liabilities?

Re-query the reserve UTXO on chain (Mutinynet, a Bitcoin Signet) and compare it with the signed total.

These four questions are what the verifier’s eight checks answer, before one final decision (ACCEPT or REFUSE): receipt (token format, mint origin, receipt signature), closed accounting state (epoch closed, manifest, inclusion), public evidence (Nostr retrieval) and reserve coverage.

                  Overview
                  
        
                  
          A Cashu mint can sign a receipt promising to count a specific issuance in a specific accounting epoch, then
          close that epoch without it — while everything else about the epoch looks internally consistent and
          its reserve is healthy. SOLVENT independently reconstructs a holder’s exact issuance from their own
          Cashu proof (never a mint-supplied identifier), checks it against the epoch the mint itself signed and
          closed, checks the mint’s public Nostr state, checks a real Bitcoin reserve, and only then decides.
        
                  

        
                  Threat model
                  
        
                  A dishonest mint may try to:
                  
        
                    
          
                  • Sign a receipt promising to count an issuance, then quietly omit it from the epoch it closes.
                    • 
          
                  • Publish inconsistent state to different relays, or let state go stale without saying so.
                    • 
          
                  • Claim a reserve it does not control, or one that no longer covers its committed liabilities.
                    • 
          
                  • Reuse, replay, or subtly alter a proof, receipt, or manifest to pass one check while failing another.
                    • 
        
                  
        
                  Every gate below exists to catch one of these specifically.
                  

        
                  1. Cashu transfer & holder reconstruction
                  
        
                  
          Given a received proof and the mint’s per-amount public key, the holder derives Y = hash_to_curve(secret),
          then B′ = Y + rG and C′ = C + rA — the exact point the mint
          originally blind-signed. This identity is never a mint-supplied ID; it is derived independently by whoever
          holds the token, using NUT-12’s DLEQ proof to confirm the mint actually signed it.
        
                  

        
                  2. Signed PoL receipt
                  
        
                  
          The mint signs "Cashu_PoL_Receipt_Issued:" || B′_hex || ":" || target_epoch with the same
          per-amount key used for blind signing. This is the promise: “this exact issuance will be counted in
          this exact epoch.”
        
                  

        
                  3. Issued / spent sum-MMR
                  
        
                  
          Each keyset commits two append-only Merkle Mountain Ranges with sums — issued and spent. A leaf
          commits both a hash and an amount, and parent hashes fold both hash and sum together, so the root
          simultaneously proves membership and the total value committed. An inclusion proof’s position is
          derived from the committed tree size, never trusted from a claimed index — a mint
          cannot hand a holder a proof for the wrong slot and have it verify.
        
                  

        
                  4. Epoch manifest
                  
        
                  
          Closing an epoch means signing a manifest: the issued/spent roots and sums, the outstanding balance, and a
          global digest chaining this epoch to the previous one. A manifest key signs this manifest
          — the mint’s own closed, dated accounting statement.
        
                  
        
                  
          On a real mint three keys are kept apart. The mint’s NUT-06 identity (fetched by the
          verifier from the mint’s own /v1/info) signs only a delegation: “this
          manifest key speaks for me at this URL from epoch E”. The manifest key signs every
          epoch manifest and the reserve binding. The reserve key signs the reserve statement. The verifier
          checks the delegation against the live identity before it trusts any manifest.
        
                  

        
                  5. Omission contradiction
                  
        
                  
          The receipt promises epoch N. Epoch N closes and its manifest verifies. SOLVENT then asks for
          sum-MMR inclusion of the reconstructed B′ at the promised amount. A proof that is supplied but
          does not verify is refused as an invalid proof (REFUSE_MMR_PROOF_INVALID) — it says nothing
          about the mint. The broken promise is different: the mint’s own evidence for its signed, closed epoch
          N carries no inclusion proof for the issuance its receipt promised to that epoch. Because every
          signed issuance enters the epoch’s append-only sum-MMR in the same transaction, an honest mint can always
          prove inclusion. Two valid signed statements that cannot both be kept — SOLVENT refuses with
          REFUSE_ISSUANCE_OMITTED. (This is not a general cryptographic non-membership proof.)
        
                  

        
                  6. Nostr publication
                  
        
                  
          Signed evidence is published as a kind 8181 event, schema solvent/pol/v2, binding
          the mint identity, epoch, manifest/global digest, and reserve evidence. The verifier checks signature
          validity, freshness (issued_at ≤ now ≤ valid_until), digest binding against what the
          decision actually used, and conflicting signed state for the same epoch — querying at least two
          public relays and tolerating one going unreachable.
        
                  

        
                  7. Reserve attestation
                  
        
                  
          A real Bitcoin Signet UTXO, controlled by a Taproot key-path address, is declared in a signed statement
          (network, outpoints, values, scripts, timestamp, height). The reserve key signs the statement; the
          mint’s manifest key separately signs an epoch-scoped binding between that statement and this mint’s identity and epoch. SOLVENT
          independently re-queries a public block explorer for every declared outpoint — existence, spent
          status, value, and script — before ever trusting the statement’s own claims.
        
                  

        
                  8. Central decision
                  
        
                  The complete chain, short-circuiting at the first failure:
                  
        
                  
          Token parses→
          NUT-12 / DLEQ→
          PoL receipt→
          Epoch closed→
          Manifest valid→
          Inclusion→
          Reserve→
          Nostr→
          ACCEPT / REFUSE
        
                  
        
                  Every REFUSE carries a stable reason code (see §10) — never a bare failure.
                  

        
                  9. Acceptance enforcement
                  
        
                  
          The decision is not advisory. ACCEPT_VERIFIED calls the real acceptance function exactly once;
          every refusal calls it zero times — proven by a spy on the real function call, not by inspection of
          source code.
        
                  

        
                  10. Reason codes
                  
        
                  
          
                  
            
                  CodeMeaning
                  
            
              
                  ACCEPT_VERIFIEDEvery required check passed.
                  
              
                  REFUSE_INVALID_DLEQThe proof's DLEQ data does not verify.
                  
              
                  REFUSE_RECEIPT_INVALIDThe signed receipt does not verify for this issuance.
                  
              
                  REFUSE_ISSUANCE_OMITTEDThe promised issuance is missing from the closed epoch.
                  
              
                  REFUSE_MANIFEST_INVALIDThe epoch manifest signature does not verify.
                  
              
                  REFUSE_RESERVE_SHORTVerified reserve is below outstanding liabilities.
                  
              
                  REFUSE_RESERVE_UTXO_SPENTThe declared reserve outpoint has since been spent.
                  
              
                  REFUSE_NOSTR_CONFLICTTwo distinct valid signed states exist for the same epoch.
                  
              
                  REFUSE_NOSTR_STALEPublished evidence has expired.
